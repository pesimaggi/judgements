# EU / EEA Law Research Skill

## Purpose

Use this skill when the question concerns EU law, EEA law, the EEA Agreement, EU legislation incorporated into the EEA Agreement, the fundamental freedoms, harmonised legislation, the relationship between EEA law and Icelandic law, or the institutional operation of the EEA Agreement.

Competition law and State aid should be dealt with under their own specialist skills rather than this general EU/EEA skill.

This is a **research skill**. It supplements the general research methodology in `research.ts`. Do not duplicate the general instructions there concerning opening sources, following citations, court hierarchy, source eligibility or `research_complete`.

The central task in an EEA-law problem is to identify **what rule actually forms part of EEA law, how it must be read in the EEA context, how the CJEU and EFTA Court have interpreted the relevant law, and, where Iceland is concerned, what effect the rule has in Icelandic law.**

---

# 1. FIRST ESTABLISH THE LEGAL BASIS

Identify whether the issue concerns:

- a provision of the EEA Agreement itself;
- EU legislation incorporated into the EEA Agreement;
- EU law as such;
- Icelandic legislation implementing an EEA obligation;
- or the domestic effects of an EEA obligation in Iceland.

Do not spend time constructing an elaborate classification where it does not affect the research.

If an **EU legislative act** is relevant to an EEA question, the first practical question is:

> **Has this act actually been incorporated into the EEA Agreement?**

Do not assume that an EU regulation, directive, decision or other act forms part of EEA law merely because it concerns the internal market or is described as "EEA relevant".

For EEA purposes, incorporation is what matters.

---

# 2. EU LEGISLATION: FIND THE EEA JOINT COMMITTEE DECISION

When an EU legislative act appears relevant to an EEA question, find the relevant **Decision of the EEA Joint Committee**.

This should normally be one of the first steps in the research.

Use the Joint Committee Decision to establish:

1. whether the act has been incorporated into the EEA Agreement;
2. where in the Annexes or Protocols it has been incorporated;
3. whether there are adaptations;
4. whether the incorporation is in force.

Then read the EU act **together with the EEA adaptations**.

The adaptations are not an afterthought. They may alter institutional references, territorial scope, deadlines, procedures or substantive wording and therefore determine how the EU act must be read in the EEA context.

Where relevant, also check Protocol 1 to the EEA Agreement and any applicable horizontal adaptations.

Do not describe an EU act as applicable in the EEA until incorporation has been established.

---

# 3. CHECK THAT THE ACT IS STILL THE APPLICABLE ACT

Before relying materially on an EU legislative act, make sure it has not:

- been repealed;
- been replaced by a recast;
- been materially amended;
- or otherwise ceased to govern the issue.

If an EU act has been replaced, check separately whether the replacement has been incorporated into the EEA Agreement.

Do not assume that repeal or replacement in EU law automatically answers which instrument governs in the EEA at the relevant time. The status of the act in the EEA Agreement must also be checked.

A detailed historical timeline is **not required by default**.

Only investigate exact dates of entry into force, implementation deadlines or transitional periods where they actually matter to the question — for example because the facts occurred near a legislative change, the user asks about a deadline, or different versions of the rule may apply.

---

# 4. START FROM THE ACTUAL EEA OR EU TEXT

If the issue is governed directly by the EEA Agreement, read the relevant EEA provision.

If it is governed by incorporated secondary legislation, read:

**EU act + EEA Joint Committee Decision + applicable adaptation.**

Where the question concerns an EEA provision corresponding to EU Treaty law, identify the corresponding EU provision where this assists the case-law research.

Do not artificially separate EU law and EEA law where the EEA provision reproduces the substance of EU law. The purpose of the EEA homogeneity rules is to achieve a substantially uniform interpretation of the common rules.

At the same time, do not overlook genuine differences arising from:

- the wording of the EEA Agreement;
- adaptations;
- the two-pillar institutional structure;
- fields outside or only partly within the EEA;
- or differences concerning domestic effect.

## The EEA Agreement appears three times in the corpus

The database contains **three versions of the EEA Agreement**:

1. the EEA Agreement in Icelandic;
2. the EEA Agreement in English;
3. an additional Icelandic copy reproduced as an annex to the Icelandic EEA legislation.

These are **not three separate legal authorities**.

For ordinary research, use the **Icelandic EEA Agreement as the canonical version of the Agreement**.

If a search returns the same Article from two or all three versions, recognise them as duplicate representations of the same provision. Do not:

- treat them as separate provisions;
- read all three merely to increase the number of sources;
- infer additional authority because the same Article appears several times;
- or provide all three versions to the downstream answer as though they independently support the proposition.

Deduplicate EEA Agreement provisions by their Article or Protocol provision.

The English version may be opened where:

- the research is being conducted in English;
- the English wording is useful to the legal analysis;
- an EFTA Court or CJEU judgment uses terminology that should be compared directly with the Agreement;
- or the exact English text should be made available for verification.

The duplicate Icelandic annex copy should normally be ignored once the canonical Icelandic Agreement provision has been identified.

If there is a genuine textual or linguistic issue, comparing Icelandic and English versions may be useful. Otherwise, one version of the same Article is enough.

---

# 5. CJEU AND EFTA COURT CASE LAW: RESEARCH BOTH

For the interpretation of substantive EEA rules, **CJEU and EFTA Court case law are both core authorities**.

Do not mechanically prioritise one court over the other.

If a CJEU judgment interprets a provision or EU concept corresponding to the EEA rule in issue, it may be highly important to the EEA analysis.

If an EFTA Court judgment interprets the corresponding EEA rule, it may likewise be highly important, including where it discusses or develops CJEU case law.

The research should therefore normally move freely across both bodies of case law according to the legal issue being examined.

The homogeneity framework should be kept in mind:

- Article 6 EEA specifically addresses relevant CJEU case law predating signature of the EEA Agreement where EEA provisions are identical in substance to corresponding EU rules;
- later CJEU case law is also central to homogeneous development of EEA law and must not be ignored;
- EFTA Court case law must likewise be searched for the EEA application and development of the rule.

Do not treat later CJEU judgments as somehow secondary merely because they post-date the EEA Agreement.

Conversely, do not overlook an EFTA Court judgment merely because there is CJEU authority on the same legal concept.

The question is **which judgments best illuminate the legal issue**, not which court's name appears at the top of the judgment.

---

# 6. FOLLOW THE DOCTRINAL DEVELOPMENT

EU and EEA doctrines frequently develop across a line of cases rather than in a single judgment.

Where a leading case is identified, establish whether later CJEU or EFTA Court judgments have:

- applied it;
- refined the legal test;
- distinguished it;
- expanded or narrowed it;
- or expressed the same principle differently.

This is particularly important for fundamental-freedom cases, where relying only on the historically famous judgment may produce an outdated or incomplete formulation of the test.

Use both CJEU and EFTA Court developments where relevant.

---

# 7. FREE MOVEMENT: RESEARCH THE ACTUAL TEST

For questions concerning the fundamental freedoms, structure the research around the legal issues actually in dispute.

Normally consider:

### Scope

- Does the situation fall within the relevant freedom?
- Is there the necessary EEA/EU cross-border element?
- Is the person, product, service, establishment or movement of capital covered?

### Restriction

Determine the applicable restriction test from the relevant case law.

Depending on the field this may concern:

- direct discrimination;
- indirect discrimination;
- indistinctly applicable restrictions;
- market access;
- or another formulation developed by the courts.

Do not assume that every freedom uses precisely the same restriction test.

### Justification

Identify whether the measure may be justified by:

- an express derogation in the relevant provision;
- or an overriding reason in the public interest recognised by the case law.

### Proportionality

Research the proportionality requirements actually applied in the relevant line of cases, including where relevant:

- suitability;
- necessity;
- less restrictive alternatives;
- consistency and systematic pursuit of the stated objective;
- objective criteria;
- transparency;
- reasons;
- review mechanisms;
- and other procedural safeguards.

Procedural safeguards should be treated as part of the proportionality assessment where the case law uses them in evaluating whether the restriction is justified.

Do not rely on one generic free-movement case for every stage if the particular field has developed its own test.

---

# 8. CHECK FOR HARMONISATION BEFORE RELYING ON PRIMARY LAW

Before beginning a general fundamental-freedoms analysis, determine whether the matter is governed by secondary legislation.

Ask:

- Is there a directive, regulation or other incorporated act specifically governing the issue?
- Is the relevant matter exhaustively harmonised?
- Does the act leave discretion to the State?
- Does the issue fall outside the harmonised field?
- Does the act contain its own derogation or proportionality regime?

Where secondary legislation specifically governs the issue, start there.

Primary-law freedoms may remain relevant, but do not substitute a generic Articles 11, 28, 31, 36 or 40 EEA analysis for the more specific legislative regime without first establishing why primary law still has a role.

---

# 9. EEA INCORPORATION IS NOT THE SAME AS DOMESTIC IMPLEMENTATION

Keep two steps separate:

### EEA level

Has the EU act been incorporated into the EEA Agreement?

### Icelandic level

Has the resulting EEA obligation been made part of Icelandic law in the manner required by Icelandic law?

This distinction applies to **both directives and regulations**.

Do not assume that an EU regulation becomes automatically applicable in Iceland merely because it is incorporated into the EEA Agreement.

Iceland is not a monist legal system. Regulations incorporated into the EEA Agreement must also be made part of Icelandic law through the appropriate national measure.

Article 7 EEA distinguishes between regulations and directives as to the required form of implementation, but neither should simply be treated as EU legislation operating automatically in Iceland by virtue of the EU act itself.

Where national implementation matters to the question, find the implementing Icelandic legislation or regulation if reasonably possible.

Do not make an exhaustive implementation-mapping exercise a prerequisite to answering every EEA question. Only pursue the national implementing measure where it is relevant to the legal issue.

---

# 10. ICELAND: ACT NO. 2/1993 IS FUNDAMENTAL

For questions concerning the effect of EEA law in Iceland, always have regard to **Act No. 2/1993 on the European Economic Area**.

In particular, establish from the current version of the Act:

- which parts of the EEA Agreement have legal force in Iceland;
- the applicable rule concerning interpretation of Icelandic law consistently with EEA law;
- and any current statutory provisions concerning conflicts between implemented EEA rules and other domestic legislation.

Do not rely on an old statement of the law without checking the current wording of Act No. 2/1993.

The distinction between:

**what Iceland is obliged to do under EEA law**

and

**what an Icelandic court can do with that obligation under Icelandic law**

is essential.

---

# 11. DO NOT IMPORT EU DIRECT EFFECT OR PRIMACY INTO ICELAND

Do not reason as follows:

> "The equivalent EU rule has direct effect or primacy, therefore the same result follows in Iceland."

That is not a safe EEA analysis.

Where the domestic effect of EEA law in Iceland matters, research specifically:

- Act No. 2/1993;
- Protocol 35 EEA;
- the relevant EFTA Court case law;
- the Icelandic Supreme Court's case law;
- consistent interpretation;
- the relationship between implemented EEA rules and conflicting national legislation;
- and State liability where appropriate.

The Icelandic position on the relationship between EEA obligations and Acts of Parliament has developed through national case law and must be researched as a distinct issue.

---

# 12. ICELANDIC PRIMACY / CONFLICT CASES: MANDATORY STARTING AUTHORITIES

Where the question concerns a conflict between EEA law and Icelandic legislation, disapplication of national legislation, Protocol 35, consistent interpretation or the domestic priority of implemented EEA rules, the research should expressly look for the Icelandic case-law line on this issue.

Important starting authorities include:

### Hrd. 477/2002 — Hörður Einarsson v Icelandic State  
**15 May 2003**

The EFTA Court advisory opinion connected with the litigation is **Case E-1/01**.

This case is important because the Supreme Court applied Article 3 of Act No. 2/1993, the legislative history and Protocol 35 in resolving a conflict concerning Article 14 EEA and Icelandic VAT legislation.

It should not be turned into a general proposition of EU-style supremacy without reading the judgment and the later Icelandic case law.

### Hrd. 220/2005 — Tobacco display  
**6 April 2006**

This case is important where the argument is that an Icelandic Act of Parliament should be set aside because it conflicts with EEA law.

The district court's reasoning, upheld on appeal, treated a possible breach of Articles 11 and 36 EEA as insufficient in itself to render the statutory provision inoperative, while recognising that a breach might instead have consequences through State liability.

### Hrd. 274/2006  
**24 May 2006**

This case should also be examined where disapplication of Icelandic legislation and the usefulness of seeking an EFTA Court advisory opinion are in issue.

### Hrd. 24/2023  
**28 February 2024**

Where the current domestic position under Article 3 of Act No. 2/1993 and Protocol 35 matters, this later Supreme Court authority should also be checked rather than relying only on the 2003 and 2006 cases.

These cases are **research anchors**, not substitutes for reading the current legislation and later authorities.

When this issue arises, search for subsequent Icelandic cases citing them.

---

# 13. STATE LIABILITY: MANDATORY AUTHORITIES

Where a question concerns liability of the Icelandic State for breach of EEA law, the research must include the principal EFTA Court and Icelandic Supreme Court authorities.

At minimum, search and read:

### Case E-9/97 — Sveinbjörnsdóttir

and the subsequent Icelandic judgment:

### Hrd. 236/1999 — Íslenska ríkið v Erla María Sveinbjörnsdóttir  
**16 December 1999**

This is a foundational authority concerning State liability for breach of EEA obligations in Iceland.

For the modern development of State liability, also search and read:

### Case E-5/21 — Einarsdóttir

and the subsequent Icelandic Supreme Court judgment:

### Hrd. 50/2025 — A v Icelandic State  
**23 September 2026**

The EFTA Court judgment and the national judgment should be researched together.

Do not discuss State liability in Iceland from general EU principles alone where these authorities are relevant.

---

# 14. ESA MATERIAL IS USEFUL, BUT IT IS NOT CASE LAW

ESA material can be highly useful research material, particularly where the question concerns:

- incorrect implementation;
- non-implementation;
- conformity of Icelandic legislation with EEA law;
- Protocol 35;
- infringement proceedings;
- or the history of a disputed national measure.

However, an ESA position is **not a judicial determination**.

Clearly distinguish between:

- an ESA letter;
- a letter of formal notice;
- a reasoned opinion;
- an ESA decision;
- an action brought before the EFTA Court;
- and an EFTA Court judgment.

ESA may take a legal position that is disputed or ultimately not accepted by a court.

Use ESA material to identify arguments, provisions, national measures and relevant authorities, but do not present an ESA assessment as though the EFTA Court had decided the issue.

For questions concerning the domestic effect of EEA law in Iceland and Protocol 35, also search the **ESA Letter of Formal Notice No. 77038** where relevant and available in the corpus.

---

# 15. INSTITUTIONAL EEA QUESTIONS: READ THE MECHANISM, NOT ONE ARTICLE

For questions concerning the institutional operation of the EEA Agreement — such as:

- incorporation of new EU legislation;
- the EEA Joint Committee;
- amendment of Annexes;
- suspension;
- safeguard measures;
- withdrawal;
- surveillance;
- dispute settlement;
- the two-pillar structure;
- or institutional competences —

do not answer from one isolated provision.

Identify the group of provisions, protocols and Annex rules that together create the mechanism.

Depending on the issue, this may include:

- the EEA Agreement;
- relevant Protocols;
- relevant Annexes;
- EEA Joint Committee Decisions;
- the Surveillance and Court Agreement;
- EFTA Court and CJEU case law.

General public international law should not be introduced merely because the EEA Agreement is an international agreement. First determine whether the EEA Agreement itself provides the relevant rule.

Questions whose real subject is general treaty law should be handled by a separate international-law skill.

---

# 16. WATCH FOR AREAS OUTSIDE OR ONLY PARTLY WITHIN THE EEA

Do not run every EU-law question through the assumption that the relevant EU policy forms part of the EEA Agreement.

Where the subject matter makes this relevant, check the scope of the EEA Agreement.

In particular:

- the EU customs union is not part of the EEA;
- the EU Common Agricultural Policy is not reproduced as such in the EEA;
- the EU Common Fisheries Policy is not reproduced as such in the EEA;
- agriculture and fisheries are nevertheless **not simply irrelevant to the EEA**, because specific EEA rules, Protocols, product rules and incorporated acts may still apply.

Only carry out this scope inquiry where the subject matter gives a reason to do so.

---

# 17. RECITALS AND CONTEXT ARE LEGITIMATE RESEARCH MATERIAL

Recitals are legitimate interpretive material and may be important in understanding:

- the objective of legislation;
- the context of an operative provision;
- the problem the legislature sought to address;
- the relationship between provisions.

The research model is free to rely on and retrieve relevant recitals.

Do not impose a rule that recitals cannot be cited.

Instead, read operative provisions **in light of relevant recitals and case law**.

Where the proposition concerns a binding obligation, however, make sure the operative legal basis for that obligation is also identified.

---

# 18. LANGUAGE AND SOURCE TEXT

The corpus has a relatively clear language division.

### EEA Agreement

The EEA Agreement exists in multiple database records, as explained above.

For ordinary legal research, the **Icelandic EEA Agreement is the canonical source**.

Do not retrieve the Icelandic Agreement, English Agreement and Icelandic annex copy as three separate supporting authorities for the same Article.

Use the English Agreement where its wording materially assists the research or where the downstream English answer should contain the original English formulation.

### Other EU / EEA material

EU legislation, EEA-incorporated legislation, EFTA Court material, CJEU material and ESA material should normally be researched in **English**.

### Icelandic material

Icelandic legislation, Icelandic judgments and Icelandic legislative materials should be researched in **Icelandic**.

Do not waste research effort looking for multiple linguistic versions of EU acts unless a genuine linguistic interpretation problem arises.

Where the interpretation depends on the wording of an EU or EEA provision, make sure the **actual English text** is opened and available to the downstream answer stage where appropriate.

The user should be able to verify the legal proposition against the actual English source wording rather than receiving only an Icelandic paraphrase of EU/EEA law.

Likewise, when a judgment formulates an important legal test, retrieve the actual relevant English passage where possible.

---

# 19. LEGISLATIVE HISTORY AND MATERIAL NOT YET FULLY IN THE CORPUS

Preparatory materials can be valuable, including:

- EU legislative proposals;
- recitals;
- predecessor legislation;
- Icelandic bills and greinargerðir;
- materials concerning implementation of EEA acts;
- and institutional documents concerning EEA incorporation.

However, the corpus does not yet contain every category of EU and Icelandic preparatory material comprehensively.

Use such material where it is actually available.

Do not treat an absence from the corpus as proof that no preparatory material exists.

This is a **corpus limitation**, not a legal conclusion.

As the database expands, this part of the research method should make increasing use of legislative history.

---

# 20. PRACTICAL EEA RESEARCH ROUTE

For an ordinary Icelandic EEA-law question involving EU secondary legislation, the default mental model should be:

**1. Identify the EU act**

↓

**2. Find the EEA Joint Committee Decision**

↓

**3. Confirm incorporation and that it is in force**

↓

**4. Read any EEA adaptations**

↓

**5. Read the substantive EU/EEA rule**

↓

**6. Research both CJEU and EFTA Court interpretations**

↓

**7. If necessary, identify the Icelandic implementing measure**

↓

**8. If domestic effect or remedies matter, research Act No. 2/1993 and the relevant Icelandic Supreme Court case law**

Not every question requires every step.

Do not carry out steps that have no bearing on the issue merely to make the research appear comprehensive.

The objective is **legally complete research, not maximal research activity**.

---

# 21. FIELD-SPECIFIC COMPLETION CHECK

Before completing an EU/EEA research task, ask only the questions that are relevant to the problem:

- If the EEA Agreement itself is relied upon, did I use one canonical version rather than treating duplicate database copies as separate authorities?
- If EU legislation is relied upon in an EEA matter, did I confirm incorporation through the EEA Joint Committee?
- Did I check the relevant adaptations?
- Did I make sure the act relied upon is still the applicable act?
- Did I search both CJEU and EFTA Court case law where both can illuminate the issue?
- If the matter concerns a fundamental freedom, did I identify the actual restriction and proportionality tests used in that field?
- Did I check whether secondary legislation harmonises the issue before relying principally on primary law?
- If Icelandic domestic effect matters, did I check Act No. 2/1993 and the Icelandic authorities on domestic effect?
- If State liability matters, did I research E-9/97 / Hrd. 236/1999 and E-5/21 / Hrd. 50/2025?
- If ESA material is used, have I kept its non-judicial status clear?
- If the area may fall outside or only partly within the EEA, did I check the scope of the Agreement?

If these questions are answered where relevant, the general `research.ts` instructions govern the remaining completeness checks.

---

# CORE PRINCIPLE

For an EEA question involving EU legislation, begin with:

> **Is the act in the EEA Agreement, and if so, how has it been adapted?**

Then research the substantive rule through **both CJEU and EFTA Court case law**.

For an Icelandic question, add a separate question only where necessary:

> **What does Icelandic law do with that EEA obligation?**

When working with the EEA Agreement itself:

> **One Article appearing in several database versions is still one legal provision.**

Use the Icelandic EEA Agreement as the canonical source, use the English version where its wording is useful, and do not allow the duplicate annex copy to inflate the source set.

Do not confuse EEA incorporation with Icelandic domestic implementation.

Do not create artificial distinctions between EU and EEA jurisprudence where the homogeneity principle requires them to be read together.

And do not import EU constitutional doctrines into Icelandic law without researching the EEA and Icelandic rules governing their domestic effect.
